Deliberate Practice

Deliberate practice is not simply doing something repeatedly. It is a structured form of improvement that requires focused attention, immediate feedback, and consistent effort at the edge of your current ability.

Three characteristics separate deliberate practice from mere repetition: it targets a specific aspect of performance, it demands full concentration, and it includes feedback so you know what to adjust.

Research across fields from music to athletics to software development confirms that the quality of practice matters far more than the quantity. An hour of focused, feedback-driven work outperforms an afternoon of passive repetition.

Grid vs. Flexbox

Grid and Flexbox solve different problems. Grid excels at two-dimensional placement — rows and columns that need to align together. Flexbox excels at distributing items along a single axis.

Reaching for the wrong one doesn't usually break a layout outright, but it tends to produce extra wrapper elements, magic numbers, or alignment that only works by accident.

A useful habit: before writing any layout CSS, ask whether the content needs to align across two axes at once. If yes, it's a Grid problem.

Debugging Layout Problems

Most layout bugs come from one of three sources: a misunderstood box model property, an ancestor with an unexpected overflow or positioning value, or a stacking context nobody meant to create.

DevTools' layout inspector will show grid tracks and flex lines directly on the page, which is usually faster than guessing from the CSS alone.

When something "should" work and doesn't, check the ancestor chain first. The element you're looking at is rarely the one causing the problem.
